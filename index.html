Consolidata
Problem System Innovation Team Documents

Every number, traceable to the cell it came from.

Consolidata reads documents arriving from departments and subsidiaries in any format, with no fixed template. It maps them to the organization’s common data model, validates them against accounting rules, and serves them as a single structured source.

Read the project draft How it works

Much of a company’s data lives outside its ERP

Organizations run standard processes such as purchasing, bookkeeping and financial consolidation on central ERP systems. Yet a large share of their data still sits in Excel sheets, PDF reports, Word documents, scanned papers and emails.

Today these documents are combined by hand: headers are matched one by one, units and formats are fixed, consistency is checked. The work is slow, depends on specific people and is prone to error. The origin of a figure is often lost along the way, which in financial reporting is a direct audit risk.

Ad-hoc report requests

When management asks several units for the same report, each one sends it in its own format, with its own headers and units.

Different system landscapes

Units on different software, or on no central system at all, send their data to headquarters as files.

Documents that never enter a system

Signed payment orders, bank statements, contracts and guarantee letters usually remain documents only.

From upload to report in four stages

The platform is built on a single processing pipeline that runs from uploading a document to presenting output to the user. Each stage passes its results forward together with their source.

  1. Ingestion

    Documents are tagged with their source unit, company and period. The original file is archived unchanged.

  2. Extraction

    Text, tables and page layout are extracted by document type. Digital and scanned documents are processed separately; tables, paragraphs and footnotes are told apart.

  3. Mapping and validation

    Line items are mapped to the common data model; currency, scale, date and number formats are normalized, and financial consistency rules are applied.

  4. Output

    The structured data powers consolidated reports, analysis views and, in a later phase, natural language queries.

Scope. The infrastructure is domain-independent; financial and accounting data is the first application area. Sales, inventory and HR can be added as separate modules on the same foundation. Consolidata does not replace the ERP. It works alongside it and structures the data the ERP does not cover.

What we do differently

OCR, layout analysis and LLM-based extraction are not new on their own. The novelty lies in combining them inside a validation and learning loop designed for financial reporting.

Template-free, contextual schema mapping
A line item’s meaning is inferred not only from its header but from neighboring columns, data types and sample values. Is “Total Revenue” net or gross?
A mapping memory that learns
Verified mappings are stored semantically. The language model runs only on the few ambiguous items the memory cannot resolve, so the system grows more consistent and cheaper with use.
Validation with accounting rules
Subtotals, balance sheet equality and scale consistency are checked automatically, catching both extraction errors and calculation errors in the source before they reach a consolidated report.
Cell-level traceability
Every value is stored with its source file, page or cell position and a confidence score. The original of any corrected value always stays accessible.
Cross-document inconsistency detection
Different names for the same concept (“Ciro”, “Net Satışlar”, “Revenue”), shifting scales and currencies, inconsistent period labels, duplicate records and missing fields are flagged before data is merged.
Natural language queries
Users type their question instead of writing queries or building filters, and get answers linked back to the source documents.
On-premise operation
Open-source local models process data without it leaving the organization, making the platform viable under data protection law (KVKK) and sector regulations.

Same concept, different meaning by sector

The hard part is not reading a document but interpreting what a value means and mapping it correctly. The Turkish Uniform Chart of Accounts (Tek Düzen Hesap Planı) serves as a shared reference: sector-specific items are linked first to account groups, then to the organization’s reporting model.

SectorTypical documents and dataWhat makes mapping hardEffect on cash flow
Construction and real estateProgress payment reports, rent and property tables, subcontractor agreementsRevenue is recognized by percentage of completion; progress billing and collection are separate conceptsAdvances and collection delays create large deviations
EnergyProduction and sales reports, long-term contractsSome amounts are FX-based; exchange rate and index clauses are buried in contract textFuture inflows depend on indices and exchange rates
TourismOccupancy and revenue reports, agency reconciliationsStrong seasonality; period definitions and prepayments varyCash inflows concentrate in certain months
Mining and manufacturingProduction, inventory and export documentsPhysical units and monetary values are intertwined; exports are in foreign currencyLetter of credit and export payment terms are decisive
Logistics and tradeInvoice lists, account statementsThe same customer appears under different trade names; record volume is highReceivables aging is decisive
Finance (banking, factoring)Credit and risk reportsRegulatory reporting formats; personal data restrictionsData cannot leave the institution

Mixed Turkish and English headers, abbreviations, the Turkish number format (1.234,56) and varying scale units are shared requirements across every sector.
